You can have the fluency, the build experience, the governance pack and the ROI model and still not get the seat. The last gap is the one nobody else teaches: how to position yourself, win the role, negotiate it and lead from day one.

This pillar treats landing the CAIO seat as its own discipline. It is the part of the journey where everything you have built becomes a narrative a board says yes to — and then becomes a plan you execute.

The first lesson of this pillar is free.

## What you'll learn

- How to build your CAIO narrative — the positioning that takes your background as a CHRO, COO, CMO, CFO or strategy leader and frames it as the qualification it is.
- How to write the board memo and the executive case: the document that makes the argument for AI leadership and for you as the person to provide it.
- How to benchmark CAIO compensation and negotiate it. Enterprise CAIO comp commonly lands in the $350K to $650K-plus range, and you will learn to position yourself within and above it.
- How to write a 100-day plan that a board trusts — what to assess, what to ship and what to leave alone in the first quarter.
- How to build your team and your budget: what to ask for, how to staff the function and how to fund it.
- How to defend all of it under pressure in a live board simulation.

## The artifact you'll build

You will produce a board-ready **CAIO pitch and 100-day plan** — and then defend it in a live board simulation, presenting to a panel acting as a board and fielding the questions a real board will ask. It is the closest rehearsal you can get for both the interview and your first real board meeting.

This is the capstone. It pulls every prior artifact — your vendor map, your built tools, your governance pack, your ROI model and adoption roadmap — into one case and forces you to stand behind it.

## How this maps to the job

The CAIO role exists because boards have decided AI is a leadership problem. CAIO adoption tripled from 26% to <span class="pl-stat">76%</span> in a year, which means search committees and CEOs are actively deciding who fills these seats right now. This pillar is built for that moment.

It is also where the convergence pays off. IBM found that 77% of CEOs see talent and technology leadership converging and 59% expect the CHRO to gain influence. Your job in the board simulation is to embody that convergence — to be the leader who can speak to the build and the workforce, the value and the risk, in one coherent case.

You leave this pillar with a pitch you have already defended, a plan you can execute and the narrative that turns "I run things" into "I should run AI here."
