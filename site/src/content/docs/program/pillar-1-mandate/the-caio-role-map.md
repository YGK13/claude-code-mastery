"Chief AI Officer" is one title covering at least five different jobs. The fastest way to feel unqualified is to compare yourself against all five at once. The fastest way to get hired is to pick the one that fits the experience you already have, and build toward it deliberately.

Below are the five archetypes, drawn from real CAIO and Head-of-AI job descriptions across financial services, healthcare, retail, technology and the public sector. Most real roles are a blend, but almost every posting leans clearly toward one.

## The five archetypes

### 1. The Strategist
Owns the enterprise AI strategy and the portfolio of bets. Reports to the CEO, sits with the executive committee, sets priorities and kills the projects that will not pay off. Measured on whether AI moves the business, not on shipping models.

*Natural fit for:* strategy leaders, COOs, general managers, consultants and operating partners. The work is prioritization, capital allocation and executive persuasion — your home turf.

### 2. The Governor
Owns AI governance, risk, ethics and compliance. Builds the policy, the review process and the committee. Answers the board's first question — "are we safe?" — and keeps the company on the right side of the EU AI Act, NIST and sector regulators.

*Natural fit for:* legal, risk, compliance and HR leaders. With AI-literacy obligations under EU AI Act Article 4 live from August 2026, this archetype is moving from nice-to-have to mandatory.

### 3. The Platform Builder
Owns the technical AI platform — infrastructure, data, tooling, the build-vs-buy decisions, the engineering org. The most technical archetype, and the one where existing data and engineering leaders have the edge.

*Natural fit for:* technical leaders rounding out their business and board skills. This is the one archetype where a non-technical leader leads through others rather than personally.

### 4. The Transformer
Owns adoption. Runs the change-management program that moves an organization from 25% AI use to a culture of fluency. Builds the literacy, the proof cases and the accountability structures that make behavior change real.

*Natural fit for:* CHROs, transformation leaders and CMOs. This is the archetype the data says matters most — 83% of CEOs say success depends more on people's adoption than on the technology — and the one most CAIO hires are weakest at.

### 5. The Commercializer
Owns AI-driven products and revenue. Turns AI capability into customer-facing offerings and new lines of business. Measured on top-line growth.

*Natural fit for:* product, GTM and commercial leaders, and founders.

## How to use the map

Three moves:

1. **Pick your lead archetype.** Which one most closely matches the experience a board would already trust you with? That is your wedge. You do not need to be credible at all five — you need to be undeniable at one and conversant in the rest.

2. **Name your adjacent strength.** Most strong CAIOs pair two archetypes. A CHRO who is a Transformer can credibly add Governor. A strategy leader who is a Strategist can credibly add Commercializer. Your pair is your positioning.

3. **Locate your gap.** Whatever archetype you choose, the role will require fluency in the others. The Transformer still has to defend a roadmap. The Strategist still has to answer the governance question. That required-but-missing fluency is the 20% the rest of this program builds.

## The pattern worth noticing

Four of the five archetypes are fundamentally about leadership, judgment and people — not about writing code. The Platform Builder is the exception, and even that role leads engineers rather than replacing them. The market is short on Strategists, Governors and Transformers who are also fluent in the technology. That is the convergence, and it is exactly the candidate an experienced leader can become.
