This audit exists to replace a feeling with a map. The feeling — "I am not technical enough to be a Chief AI Officer" — is almost always wrong, but it is too vague to argue with. The map is specific, and specific is something you can act on.

Score yourself honestly on each of the twelve competencies below, on a scale of 1 (no real exposure) to 5 (I have done this repeatedly, at a senior level). Be a tough grader. The point is not a high score. The point is to see the shape of your gap.

## The twelve competencies

**Leadership and business (the 80% you likely already hold)**

1. **Executive influence** — persuading a board or C-suite to back a direction, and being trusted to do it.
2. **P&L and capital allocation** — owning a budget, prioritizing bets, killing what does not pay off.
3. **Change management** — moving a skeptical organization to adopt a new way of working.
4. **Cross-functional translation** — making one function understood by another, and brokering between them.
5. **Team building** — hiring, structuring and leading a function.
6. **Risk and stakeholder judgment** — reading political, legal and reputational risk, and navigating it.

**AI-specific (the 20% most candidates need to build)**

7. **AI fluency** — explaining how modern models, agents and retrieval work, well enough to defend a roadmap to a board.
8. **Build-vs-buy and vendor judgment** — evaluating AI tools, platforms and vendors on cost, risk and fit.
9. **AI governance** — designing policy, review and a governance committee aligned to the EU AI Act and NIST.
10. **AI value and ROI** — building the funding case and the system that tracks whether AI is actually paying off.
11. **Adoption design** — running the literacy and behavior-change program that gets real usage, not just access.
12. **Proof you can ship** — having personally built or deployed a working AI tool, not just commissioned one.

## Reading your result

Add your scores for competencies 1-6 (leadership) and 7-12 (AI-specific) separately.

- **Leadership 24-30, AI-specific 6-15.** This is the most common profile for an experienced executive, and it is the strongest possible starting point. You hold the unteachable 80%. You are exactly who this program is built for. Pillars II through VI close competencies 7 through 12 in order.

- **Leadership 24-30, AI-specific 16-24.** You are close. You likely need depth in two or three specific areas — most often governance, ROI or proof-you-can-ship. Use the audit to target them rather than starting from scratch.

- **AI-specific higher than leadership.** You are likely a technical leader pursuing the Platform Builder archetype. Your build is the reverse: the leadership, governance and board competencies. The program still applies — you will lean harder on Pillars IV, V and VI.

## What this tells you

Almost everyone who runs this audit honestly discovers the same thing: their leadership column is full and their AI column is thin and specific. That is not a verdict of "unqualified." It is a punch list. A punch list is a far better thing to hold than a feeling of dread, because you can finish a punch list.

The single most common gap — and the one that most separates the candidates who get the seat from the ones who do not — is competency 12, proof you can ship. It is also the most fixable. That is why this program is built around a portfolio rather than a memo.
