This is the pillar that decides whether anything you build survives. MIT found that 95% of generative-AI pilots deliver zero measurable ROI. Those pilots did not fail because the technology did not work. They failed because nobody owned value and nobody owned adoption.

A Chief AI Officer is hired to be in the 5%. This pillar is how you get there: by treating value and adoption as the actual product, and the technology as the means.

The first lesson of this pillar is free.

## What you'll learn

- Why 95% of AI efforts fail — the organizational failure modes, not the technical ones — and the specific moves that put you on the other side of that number.
- How to build an AI value and ROI model that finance will accept: where value comes from, how to size it, how to track it and how to defend it.
- How to prioritize a portfolio of AI use cases by value, feasibility and risk, so you fund the right things and kill the rest early.
- How to design the adoption program: map the roughly 25% of your people already using AI, turn them into proof cases, and scale literacy from there.
- How to design the AI operating model and the org around it — who owns what, how AI work flows and where the function reports.
- How to measure behavior change, not just usage, so you can prove adoption is real and durable.

## The artifact you'll build

You will produce two linked artifacts: an **AI Value & ROI model** and a **12-month adoption roadmap**. The first shows where the value is and how you will prove it. The second shows how you will move a workforce — quarter by quarter, from the early adopters out to the whole organization. Together they are the operating plan a CAIO presents to a board.

This is the difference between a pilot and a program.

## How this maps to the job

CAIO job descriptions ask for ownership of "enterprise AI value realization," "adoption and change management" and "AI operating-model design." This pillar produces all three as working artifacts.

This is where the Build Gap and the Wage Gap are closed in practice. The Build Gap closes when value tracking and prioritization push projects through to production instead of letting them stall as pilots. The Wage Gap closes through the adoption engine — mapping the people already using AI, building proof cases, scaling literacy and measuring behavior change. PwC measured a 56% wage premium for AI-skilled roles, and that premium only materializes for an organization whose people actually adopt. A CAIO who can move a workforce is rarer and more valuable than one who can only buy technology.

You leave this pillar able to prove value and drive adoption — the two things that separate the 5% from everyone else.
