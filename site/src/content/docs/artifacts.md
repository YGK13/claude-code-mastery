## The six artifacts

### 1. CAIO Positioning Map
**Pillar I — The mandate.** [Free — included in the foundation]

A one-page map of your CAIO thesis: the sector you serve, the wedge you own, the three problems you are the obvious hire to solve. It is the artifact that turns a generalist resume into a specific point of view. Boards do not hire generalists into AI leadership. They hire the person with the cleanest thesis.

### 2. AI Capability & Vendor Map
**Pillar II — The landscape.** [Locked — available in the Certificate]

A defensible map of the AI capability stack for your target sector, scored against the vendors that matter. The board does not want a vendor list. It wants to know you have done the work and can defend a build, buy or partner call on any layer. This is that document.

### 3. Deployed AI assistant (RAG or agent)
**Pillar III — Deployment.** [Cohort: shipped with cohort assist · Free build available in Pillar III]

A working internal AI assistant — a RAG system over a real corpus, or a narrow agent that completes a real workflow. The non-technical executive who can point at a live system they shipped is rare and obvious. A screenshot is not the artifact. The system is.

### 4. AI Governance Pack
**Pillar IV — Governance.** [Locked — available in the Certificate]

The policy, the AI council charter and the risk register a public-company GC would sign. Mapped to NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act Article 4 literacy requirement that becomes enforceable in August 2026. This is the artifact that ends the "but who owns the risk" conversation in the boardroom.

### 5. AI Value & ROI Model + 12-month adoption roadmap
**Pillar V — Value.** [Locked — available in the Certificate]

A defensible value model and a twelve-month adoption roadmap a CFO will fund. 95% of enterprise AI pilots return zero measurable ROI (MIT NANDA). The artifact is the antidote — a model that names where the money comes from, when it shows up, and what has to be true for it to land.

### 6. Board-ready CAIO pitch + 100-day plan
**Pillar VI — The operating model.** [Cohort — defended in the board simulation]

The deck and the narrative you would present in week one of the role. The 100-day plan with the three measurable wins. The org design. The first three hires. In the cohort, you defend it in a live board simulation to a panel that includes a sitting board director. You leave with the pitch and with the muscle memory of having defended it under pressure.
